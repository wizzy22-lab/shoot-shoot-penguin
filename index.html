Shoot Shoot Penguin streamlined and systemized this repetitive workflow, enabling organizers to manage games more efficiently while helping players discover and join games with ease.

[ 02 — Before the product ]

Finding players was often harder than organizing the game itself.

Every weekend, amateur basketball clubs organized games—but securing a court didn't guarantee a match. If not enough players signed up, the game couldn't happen at all. Most clubs managed recruitment through fragmented channels such as KakaoTalk chats and community forums, forcing organizers to repeat the same manual process every week just to fill remaining spots.

[ 05 — the core question]

How might we predict guest player demand in advance?

The real challenge wasn't simplifying the recruitment process. It was that organizers had to calculate player shortages manually before every game. Although attendance data accumulated over time, it was never used to predict future demand or support decision-making.

What if attendance data could predict how many guest players would be needed?

That question became the starting point for Shoot Shoot Penguin.

Attendance Patterns Historical Data Guest Demand Prediction

[ 06 — One System, Two sides]

Different Needs, One System

Although organizers and guest players had different needs, both stemmed from the same fragmented recruitment process—and could be addressed through one unified system.

A. Team Leads Needs
"How many guest players will we need for this game?"
B. Players Needs
"Where can I find a game right now?"

Attendance data predicts how many guest players will be needed, and those predictions automatically generate recruitment posts. These open games are then displayed on the guest map, making them easy to discover. Guest applications flow back into the system, continuously improving the accuracy of future predictions.
